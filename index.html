How does a city get an ambulance through when its cameras can fail and its streets are full?

An open research program on seeing traffic, simplifying road networks, and controlling signals, with every result traceable to its data.

Soheil Fallahzadeh, M.Sc. student in algorithms and computational geometry, Yazd University.

This work extends my M.Sc. thesis on machine vision, deep learning and fuzzy logic for intersection control toward network-level emergency navigation.

One question, three layers

An emergency vehicle needs three things from a city: eyes that know when they can’t be trusted, a road network simple enough to reason about quickly, and signals that react before a junction locks up. Each layer is its own repository, tested on its own terms.

The eyes

Cameras see traffic, but they get fooled by rain, glare and parked trucks. These three repositories measure how much to trust what they report.

Can the camera be trusted right now?

Perception reliability monitor. Scores a camera’s health without ground truth and tells a controller when to fall back to a fixed plan.

0.938 AUC predicting when detection is reliable (image quality alone: 0.873)

Raises the alarm within 3 to 8 seconds when the detector actually fails, and stays quiet when it copes.

Is that an ambulance, and which road is it on?

Emergency-vehicle perception for preemption. Turns camera tracks into one event a signal controller can act on: road edge, distance, arrival time, confidence.

Road matching accuracy 97.4% (nearest-road baseline: 90.0%)

Median 1.1 ms per frame on one CPU core. In simulation, preemption cut ambulance passage from 34.4 s to about 20 s.

Geo-SmartSpanner. A graph neural network predicts which roads to drop, and a repair step restores the guarantee on directed city graphs.

The reflexes

At the junction, a controller has seconds to decide. It must move traffic without blocking the intersection itself.

How do you keep a junction from locking up?

Anti-gridlock signal control. Max-pressure control with a fuzzy throttle that backs off when the road ahead is filling.

Ambulance delay 143.3 s to 125.0 s with preemption (p = 0.049)

Under chronic overload, 342.6 s average delay against 380.3 s for plain max-pressure, and about 10% less CO₂.

Open questions

The seven repositories each answer a narrow question. These are the questions they leave open, and where I want to go next.

Can a controller use the camera’s confidence, not just its count?

Reliability and queue-uncertainty scores already exist as outputs. No controller consumes them yet.

Does a certified backbone survive noisy travel times?

The certificate assumes known edge costs. Real costs are estimated, and the estimates can be wrong.

Can local preemption compose into a corridor?

Preemption is tested on one approach and one signal. A corridor needs many intersections to agree.
